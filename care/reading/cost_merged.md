# cost: merged account of readers A and B

Question put to the texts: WHAT IT COSTS THEM (beings given a name or role of care).

Sources: `cost_A_notes.md` (reader A, sample `cost_A.md`, 300 beings) and `cost_B_notes.md` (reader B, sample `cost_B.md`, 300 beings). The two samples are different beings. This file only compares the two reports; no kinds were formed here and the samples were not re-read. All counts are the readers' own, out of 300. In both reports a being can carry several kinds, so counts do not sum to 300. Every quote below was checked by script against the sample file it is cited from (A or B, then the being number). Three quotes that reader A had typed with straightened quotation marks (A 5486, A 9605, A 2586) are given here in the sample's own characters; A 2586 is given as the full sentence.

Size labels in the table: (a) found by both, similar size; (b) found by both, clearly different size; (c) found by one only. The line between (a) and (b) is drawn here at a ratio of about 1.5 between the two counts; where a pair sits near that line, the row says so.

- Explicit cost vocabulary is rare. A: the words "appear in a small minority", "About 10 beings". B: "Only 12 beings use cost, price, pay or sacrifice anywhere in their sentences."
- Much of what was selected is description that the reader model took as cost. A: "For perhaps a third of the sample the text does not treat anything as a cost". B: "Most answers are descriptions that the reader model took to be costs"; within the body kind, "about 27 of the 65" give the mark "as plain description".
