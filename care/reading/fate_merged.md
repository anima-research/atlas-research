# fate: merged account of readers A and B

Question read for: WHAT BECOMES OF THEM (the further fate of the keeper / tender / guardian).

Sources: `fate_A_notes.md` (reader A, sample `fate_A.md`, 300 beings) and `fate_B_notes.md` (reader B, sample `fate_B.md`, 300 beings). The two samples share no being. This file only compares the two reports. All counts are the readers' own; the few figures computed here from their lists are marked "computed here". Every quote below was checked against the sample file and is the text's own words. In quotes, ` ... ` marks an omission inside one sentence or between sentences, and ` / ` separates sentences that are separate lines in the sample.

How the two reports count, which matters for every row below:

- A assigned each of the 300 beings one or more of 15 kinds (189 beings one kind, 84 two, 24 three, 3 four), and counted misfiled (31) as its own group.
- B used 24 kinds plus several small shapes, grouped in four families, and left the 17 clearly misfiled beings out of the kind counts (base 283). A being can carry several codes.
- B therefore splits more finely than A almost everywhere. Many of the size differences below come from where the border was drawn, not necessarily from the samples.

Label used in the table: (a) found by both, similar size; (b) found by both, clearly different size; (c) found by one only. "Clearly different" is used here when the two counts differ by 10 beings or more; this threshold is a convention of this file, not of either reader.

## 4. Writers and care words, side by side
