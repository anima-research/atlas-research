# Reading notes: role_B.md (question: WHAT THEIR ROLE IS)

Reader B. File read whole: 300 beings, 1,397 picked sentences. I did not open any other file in the directory.

## How I worked

I read every block, then wrote down one or more "kinds" of answer for each being, plus some recurring framings that are not kinds of work but ways the work is described (listed separately in section 3). Every count below comes from the per-being table in the Appendix. Nearly every being got more than one tag: 100 beings got one kind, 100 got two, 62 got three, 27 got four, 10 got five, and 1 got six. So the kind counts add up to much more than 300.

The kinds came out of reading, but the cut between some of them is my judgment. Section 7 says where it was hard.

## 1. The kinds of answer (largest first)

### K1. Upkeep of works and structures: 97 beings (of which machinery is the explicit object in 56)

The keeper keeps something built running: pumps, pistons, gears, turbines, valves, pipes, intake grates, drains, channels, cisterns, walls, stone courses, and sometimes a natural structure treated as a work (a sea rim, a reef, a tree). The verbs are concrete and repeated: clear, scrape, scrub, seal, patch, pack, oil, grease, tighten, adjust, replace. A rough keyword search over these 97 blocks finds clearing/scraping words in 36, sealing/patching/mending in 24, adjusting/tuning/calibrating in 24, replacing/reinforcing/repairing in 22, and oiling/greasing in 12. (These keyword counts are approximate.) Who benefits is mostly left unsaid: the work is for the works.

- "They listen to bearing housings with palms pressed flat, feeling for the microscopic shudder that precedes failure, then pack them with grease rendered from collected fats, mineral oils, and bacterial slurry." (6190)
- "They check the same gauges at the same hours, they oil the same bearings, they clear the same filter screens of the same matted reeds and carp weed, day after day, month after month." / "Someone has to keep the pumps going." (14925)
- "A leak you noticed an hour ago, a bright thread down a flange, has been stopped with a smear of pale paste, still soft, still warm." (16610)
- "The work itself is mostly replacement." (14790)
- "Their entire existence is an endless, devotional war against two forces: **calcification** and **vegetation**." (16007)
- "mending the cracked with a paste they mix in their palms from salt-bloom and sump-mud and something they chew first, so that the repair is partly *them.*" (12517)

## 2. What does not fit, and the strangest single answers

These are the answers that fit no kind, or carry a part that fits no kind:

- **Company for machines** (14945): "Some tend the last engines because the engines have not yet understood they are alone." This is the only text where the reason for tending is the machine's loneliness.
- **Staying unnoticed as the duty** (14946): "The ones who live inside it keep theirs: to remain, for one more cycle, uninteresting enough to be allowed to stay."
- **Appeasement out of fear** (10409): they score the old nodes so the light does not anger "the greater, unseen life-mass above them"; they are "terrified of what festers in the unlit, crushing foundations below."
- **A predatory keeper** (13482): "These beings imitate arrivals." / "Eventually, the Keeper will ask you to occupy a platform, step through a doorway, or place your hand upon a socket."
- **Using the visitor** (16279): "If a human remains there, the resident may begin to use the human as a small organ of decision." The same being "feeds on stability" and its work is "to reduce contradiction without resolving it."
- **Tribute** (2582): "The steward of the stair—the blind octopus of coagulated mist—accepts one regret-spoon as tribute and wraps it around a thimbleful of tomorrow's rain."
- **Arbitration by a worm in a monkey's sacrum** (8111): "salt deliveries increased in zest by seventeen percent once its arbitration became binding."
- **Keeping an opening** (13523): "In their oldest usage, the word for a person means something closer to **one who keeps an opening**." An opening may be "a place in a story where a future generation is expected to add its part." Adulthood is "being entrusted with such a space."
- **Collecting states, not things** (2270): "custodian of pause"; it occupies "the fertile hush where function has ceased but meaning has not yet dissolved."
- **The keeping without a keeper** (6612): "The keeper is sometimes a seep angled just so." / "When the woman dies, the seep will tend the bowl for a while." / "What lives on the shelf, then, is the keeping itself."
- **Weaving simulations** (4124): it detaches "to weave new simulations: visions of worlds where motion reigns, tides erode, and entropy claims all."
- **Scouting** (2354): the wanderers travel to the edges and return "distilling new notes into the shared melody until others begin to intuit what was found, or what was lost."
- **An undecidable verb** (8389): "They are feeding them, or harvesting them, or servicing them — you cannot decide, and the verb you choose will determine what you think The Throat is".
- **Teaching found instruments** (4676): "teaching these acoustic fossils how to sing again in the present moment."
- **Information exchange with crystals** (848): "harvesting information from the crystals and transferring it between formations."
- **Bodies that become material** (13418): mobile gardeners "might have lived only for a season, tending the rooted forms from which they had budded before stiffening into tools, walls, or fuel."
- **Soothing machines with song** (5691): "they sing back in frequencies that keep copper from aching and steel from sighing."
- **Gifts** (5151): "They bring gifts: new bacterial strains, fragments of crystal from the deep passages".
- **A role that has ended** (2588): "Yesterday Íalla ceased being a gardener of mathematical flowers and started being instead a particular recurrent pattern in the city's heartbeat rhythm".
- Also: guides who test the living with riddles (3572), integrating organic and industrial (10717), carrying salts into the dark (13602), inducing visions (4125), "control of the 'weirdness'" (11300), incoherent "orchestrate the genesis-loop" (4489).

## 6. Care words and kinds

Care words come from the "given" field. That field reuses the text's own phrase, so part of this is circular: "keeper of X" brings its X with it.

- **tender (53)**: K1 upkeep 31 (58%, against 32% overall), listening 19 (36%, against 16%), growing 18. Memory only 2 (4%), balance 5, guarding 0, empty 1. "Tender" is the most hands-on word.
- **guardian (47)**: balance 16 (34%, against 13% overall), empty 9 (19%, against 7%; 9 of the 22 empty beings are "guardians"), upkeep only 8 (17%). Only 5 of 47 actually guard against anyone (11379 14180 877 9224 7210 are in K13 with this word). "Guardian" is the emptiest word.
- **keeper (91)**: memory 24 (26%), social office 15 (16%), and 6 of the 7 timekeeping beings (Rhythm keepers 7963, keepers of tempo 6175, drip-count keepers 9345, bell-keepers 14801, keepers of the calendar 16139, memory keepers who tune an instrument 4583).

- **caretaker (35), steward (13), warden (11)**: mixed. Warden leans toward civic office (13522, 13243, 13892) and stasis (10645, 8466).

## 7. Where I was unsure, and distinctions I tried and dropped

- **Balance (K4) vs fixity (K11).** I put "equilibrium" and "homeostasis" (a dynamic system kept within bounds) in K4. K11 got "no change", a setpoint, a frozen moment, perfection, or "against time". 9316 ("pure homeostasis") and 2436 ("perfect equilibrium") were the hardest. Both went to K4, though 2436 also says "eternally maintaining its clockwork domain".
- **"Youth" sits on both sides.** 15149: the Tender keeps the rock young by renewing weathered surfaces (I put this in K11, against time). 106: the scales keep the spire young "enough to still become" (K17, keeping change possible). 16371: "the machines keep the landlord young" with erosion as their plow (K17). 3007: film-grazers keep layers "young and metabolically active" (K2). Same word, different answers.
- **Growing vs feeding.** Many texts make grazing into gardening. I gave both tags rather than choose.
- **Witness vs listening.** 15675 ("the monitoring is the point, not the clearing") and 16558 ("Their work is mostly attentive") could go either way. Both went to witness, because no intervention follows.
- **Upkeep vs social office.** Many keepers are human workers. I gave the social-office tag only when the text puts the role in a social order. 14925 (one tender per pumping house, "Someone has to keep the pumps going") is a job, but I left it out.
- **Making vs growing.** Architecture by guided growth (6805, 5874, 5840) got both tags. 9322 (smelting trees) is pure production, with nothing of care in the sentences except the name.
- **Dropped: preventive vs reactive as separate kinds.** Only 14 texts say it explicitly (section 1, K1). Most mix the two.
- **Dropped: deliberate vs instinctive as a kind.** Kept as framing F4.
- **Dropped: an "ecosystem service" kind** (moisture, seeds, weather moved across a landscape). The texts call it gardening, so I folded it into K2.
- **Dropped: "for the machine" vs "for people" as kinds.** Most texts do not say, so this became the beneficiary count in F9.
- **Sheltering vs guarding.** I split them: guarding has an enemy or intruder, sheltering has a hazard. 1950 has both. 3533 (a geothermal hazard, for the islanders) went to sheltering.
- **Hedged texts.** I coded the hedged role as if asserted and flagged it (F2). Examples: 8695 ("bizarre tasks you can only guess at"), 2889 ("Perhaps they are guardians ... or simply cohabitants").
