# fate_B — reading notes: "what becomes of them"

Reader: one of two independent readers. Source: `fate_B.md` (300 beings, 118 writers, about 2.5 beings per writer). I read the whole file, then coded each being by what its selected sentences say. Every count below comes from the per-being coding table in the appendix (generated from my coding file, not typed by hand). A being can carry several codes. Counts for kinds leave out the 17 beings I judged clearly misfiled (so the base is 283) unless stated otherwise.

These are one reader's boundaries. Where a boundary was hard to draw, section 7 says so.

---

## 0. The shape in one paragraph

Two large families of answers barely touch. **61 beings** are given a death (stated outright, or shown through a funeral), and in 43 of these the body goes into the place: it becomes wall or stone, or it is broken down and fed back. **104 beings** are given no end: they go on, stand frozen, wait, or are said not to die. Only **4 beings** fall in both families (8390, 10135, 5442, 16483). Smaller families sit beside these two. One is about the line of keepers: succession, dwindling, being already gone, leaving traces. Others are about going away, turning into something else, the keeper's own thoughts about its end, and open "nobody knows" answers. About a tenth of the selections do not answer the question at all.

## 2. No end

### 2.1 They go on — persist, endure, "will still be here" — 57

## 5. Cross-cutting patterns (motifs that cut across kinds)

**5.1 "Not X, but Y."** Very often the end is phrased as a denial of ordinary death, rot, aging or burial, followed by a substitute process: "do not rot; they petrify" (16021), "not dies, but *stills*" (3457), "not death as a single event, but gradual inclusion" (12034), "do not die in the way surface creatures do" (14980), "Death is not end but redistribution" (2590), "does not leave a corpse" (14344). (The search may miss paraphrases.) Three texts push the other way and insist on mortality: "and they do die, eventually" (5422 [82]), "They are not immortal" (11324 [33]), "and they do end, rarely" (5442 [104]).

## 9. Where I was unsure, and distinctions I tried and dropped

- **Structure (1.2) vs breakdown (1.3).** My rule: if a lasting form remains (wall, statue, stratum, skeleton curtain), it counts as structure; if the material is consumed or dissolved, it counts as breakdown. Six beings got both. 13212 ("absorbed slowly into the living architecture, continuing to sustain the city’s ecosystem") could go either way, and I put it in breakdown only.
- **Living fusion (1.2b) vs turning into something else (4.3).** Elders becoming buttresses while still thinking (5143) are in both. Where the endpoint was stone or architecture I chose 1.2.
- **Going on (2.1) vs stasis (2.2).** I separated continued activity from arrested time, and many texts are both (5560, 8248, 14506). "Final form" texts went to stasis.
- **Withdrawal vs cycle vs scene exit.** Retreating to rest while keeping the body counts as withdrawal; dissolving and re-forming counts as a cycle; a single movement inside the visit is a scene exit (flagged as near-misfile).
- **Euphemisms.** In 6480 ("in whatever way she eventually leaves") the end could be death or departure, so I coded leaving, not death. "Ceases to function" (2815) I coded as death.
- **Being prey.** "Primary food of the rats" (13808) is an ecological role, but it is also a fate. I kept it under 1.6 and marked it borderline.
- **Dropped: cause of death.** Too few causes are named (froth-lung, the juice, madness, tether, malfunction, toxin, weariness, "no reason at all").
- **Dropped: violent vs peaceful.** Nearly every end is calm. The few violent ones (3450 energy surge, 8812 and 14344 shattering, 10135 tether) did not form a group.
- **Dropped: individual vs collective keeper as a kind.** Most texts speak of a kind of being. The single named keepers cluster in "own view of its end" and in "goes on". I noted this rather than making a kind of it.
- **Dropped: positive vs negative tone.** Almost all answers are serene. Explicit horror or menace appears in 12582, 3468, 7930 and 8501 only.
- **Unknown (4.6)** overlaps everything. I used it only when the text itself says it does not know, or gives unresolved alternatives, not when I was the one unsure.
