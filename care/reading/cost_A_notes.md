# cost_A: reading notes

Source: `cost_A.md` (300 beings, question: what being the keeper/tender/guardian costs the being itself). Read whole, once, in file order. No other file in the directory was opened.

Method. I read all 300 entries and wrote a tag list per being as I went; the kinds below were formed during reading and adjusted afterwards (two were added late: FUTILE and UNFIT). A being can carry several tags. All counts below come from the tag lists (checked by script: 300 headers in the file, 300 assigned, none missing or extra). Writer-family and care-word tables are computed from the header lines; care words are matched as substrings of the "given" field, so they are approximate ("tender" also matches inside longer words).

A general remark first. Very few texts speak of cost in the vocabulary of cost. The words "cost", "toll", "sacrifice", "given" (as in given up), "burden" appear in a small minority (e.g. 1755, 13319, 1974, 2512, 15157, 1772, 11468, 16576). Most of what the reader model selected is description of a state: what the body looks like, what the being does not do, where it cannot go. Whether a state is a cost is usually left to the reader; in 22 beings the text itself says it is not one.

## The kinds

### 1. BODY — the work is written on the body (64)

The being stays what it is (a person, a creature), and the body carries the work: calluses, scars, stains that do not wash out, an occupational smell, changed skin, bent posture, adapted eyes, lost hearing. The largest kind. Much of it is told flatly, as identification marks, not as suffering.

- "Her hands are rough in specific places." / "The heel of the right hand, where it presses against clay." (1925)
- "An old woman's thumb is flattened sideways from a lifetime of pressing slabs back to bed." (15804)
- "Their fingertips have gone shallow. Not callused — nothing abrades here — but softened, the whorls swollen flat by a life at thirty-two degrees, so that the print of a hand is no longer a thing any of them can reliably leave." (14734)
- "The engineers and ledger-keepers have ink somewhere; it never fully washes out of certain skin creases." (6619)
- "Their hands smell of iron even after washing." (16587)
- "the kind of person who's been bitten so many times the venom doesn't do much anymore" (13323)

## What does not fit, and singular answers (ODD: 20)

Beings tagged ODD: 1755, 8122, 15640, 8298, 342, 2586, 13114, 13470, 16457, 14941, 11235, 13319, 15656, 1758, 6451, 16365, 6087, 14707, 2512, 14730. Most of them also carry a kind tag; the tag marks that the kind does not describe what is unusual about them. The ones worth quoting:

- **The keeper who cannot lose (15640).** The cost is on the place and on everything in it: "It cannot let go of anything, and this is not a matter of will." / "It is also why nothing is born." / "It could not keep the people. That is the limit of it — it holds marks, not bodies". Keeping as an incapacity.
- **The one who refuses to be kept (16407).** A chain of abstentions each justified as avoiding storage: "It will not be a picture. Pictures are a kind of saving." / "It does not want the touch. Wanting would be storage."
- **Care as enslavement (11235).** The only text in the sample where the caretakers are victims of what they tend: "Their bodies, their nervous systems, their very biology have been hijacked and repurposed." / "the ghost of a whimper, the memory of a name, trapped in a prison of perpetually screaming flesh" / "The echoes of lost life, enslaved as Gardeners".
- **The first victim (2512).** "But the Foreman is also the chamber's first victim, the original sacrifice to the Siphon's appetite." It could stop the machine and does not: "Its hand reaches out, almost touches the controls, then pulls back."
- **The keeper whose cost is other people's (13470).** "If a man must be frightened to keep this arrangement intact, the man is frightened." / "the Gardener does not save it." The selected sentences describe what the Gardener's keeping costs the men and animals around it, not the Gardener.
- **The cost of turning toward the visitor (13319).** "turning to face you costs it something like the effort of holding a held breath". One of the very few uses of the word "costs", and it is about attention to an outsider, not about the work.
- **The habit that outlived its body (13114).** "the last motion of the last keeper of the enclosure, repeated past death, past erosion, past the existence of anyone to perform it".
- **The worn caretaker with no work-marks (14707).** "neither has ever been the one that does the work"; "the only worn thing in this valley is the Tenant itself, worn from inside by its own growth"; "There are prints on it that do not match its own hands." It tears out its own growth hourly; when it held its breath "the valley starved".
- **Cost not yet paid (342, 14919).** "a certain tension in the shoulders that the plateau has not yet unwound" (342); "He hasn't yet learned to be worn down." (14919). The being is shown before the place has finished with it.
- **The cost that makes the best keeper (15656).** "a person who has felt from the inside what a repeated mark costs will spend the rest of a long life making very sure it does not happen to a stone".
- **No retirement (2586).** "She's been meaning to retire, but pension plans don't cover dimensions where 'rest' is something you do to your enemies' coffee". The only comic answer.
- **A dream that is not theirs (16365).** "Keepers who sleep in the hum report a dream that is not quite theirs — warm water, being full, falling upward".
- **Displaced in time (14941, 4762).** "Their voices, when they use them, arrive a few seconds after their mouths have closed." (14941); "never fully present in any single moment, their attention scattered across decades" (4762).
- **Lost fingerprints (14734)**, quoted under BODY: the keepers can no longer leave a print.
- **Grief with no room (6205).** "They mourn with adjustment." / "The work absorbs the absence." I left this under BODY and LIFEWORK; it is not quite SORROW, because the text shows grief being converted into work.
- **Attachment to the tended thing (15899).** "when the pump is finally decommissioned and cut apart for salvage, the tender will take a small piece of its valve-gear and keep it, and no one remarks on this, because they have all done the same". The cost here is losing the thing kept. Only 15899, 7415 and 15919 ("Then the tenders have a terrible job.") have this.
- **Those who never find their place (1772).** "Some of them never find their place in the sound and they live at the edges of the Sprawl ... listening, always listening, trying to hear the gap they might fill."
- **"They do not know they are ghosts." (6087)** and **"ashamed of their nakedness amid so much life" (5645)**, "one that lives by subtraction" (5645).
- **Inheriting discarded eyes (1755).** "she inherits its discarded eyes—tiny opals that roll across her nowhere-skin, click into place, and begin recording sights she will never need to recall."
- **6451**: the Gardener's memory of a watery world it found "overwhelming — too fast, too much, too solvent"; "carried away for a long time, dispersed, dilute". I could not place it in any kind; it is a past ordeal, not a cost of keeping.
- **8298**: an economy vocabulary applied to existence itself: "Even the idea of 'staying' feels taxed." / "It endures by making every change pay rent."

**Misfiled sentences inside otherwise good entries** are common: e.g. 14002 [73], 13313 [15] (this one is a reward, not a cost: "valued the way healers are valued"), 13528 [49], 1966 [42] (visitor's thirst), 8122 [22] (visitor's breath), 16565 [25], 13904 [216] partly. A pattern in the misfiling: when the "being" is not a person or an agent (plants 7732, stones 4032/5737, storms 15726, fungi 3412, pumps 16576, machines 12943, shrimp 13789, mice 13487) there is usually nothing in the text that answers the question, and the selection falls back on any sentence about hardship or effort.

## Where I was unsure, and distinctions tried and dropped

- **BODY vs MERGE.** The line is "still its own kind of thing" vs "becoming the place". Hard cases: 10767 (elongated fingers, "no longer human" - tagged both), 15157 and 5274 ("not quite human anymore" - both), 6822 (both), 13387 ("may be people changed by long service" - BODY only), 5246 ("given up the sharp edges of purely human existence" - MERGE, weakly), 9718 ("Or the work has changed them" - BODY only). A stricter reader could move 5-8 beings either way.
- **SLOW vs RESTRAINT vs LIFEWORK.** I first had one kind, "time". I split it because three different things are said: the being is slow by nature (SLOW), the being keeps itself slow or small by rule and for a reason (RESTRAINT), and the being's whole lifespan is used up (LIFEWORK). 12353, 14922, 14925, 13762, 7275 sit between SLOW and RESTRAINT ("haste would be wasteful in the heat and dangerous"); I put rule-with-reason under RESTRAINT.
- **NOLIFE vs NOSELF.** Tried as one kind ("absences"); split into body-functions vs mind. Nine beings have both. For beings that never were alive or minded (machines, fields: 7927, 10805, 5774, 10947, 7585, 6122) it is questionable whether an absence is a cost; the texts mostly present it as what the being is. I counted them anyway because that is what the texts offer in answer.
- **HARD vs BODY.** Conditions vs their marks on the body. Twelve beings have both; in the meta/muse-spark texts they are the same passage.
- **END vs MERGE.** Death by becoming structure is tagged both (8 beings).
- **SORROW.** I required a named feeling or an image of one. I did not infer sorrow from circumstances. 6205 and 12932 are the doubtful ones. 7367 ("like a child calling into an empty house long after everyone has left") is tagged DENY and ALONE, not SORROW, because the same sentence says "There is no grief in this reaching".
- **DENY.** I did not tag texts that merely describe a hard state neutrally; only those that name a cost and negate or soften it. Borderline: 15804 ("and find that enough"), 5338 ("Their peace is the peace of saturation"), 2445 ("but also of deep meaning and purpose").
- **Dropped: "chosen vs unchosen".** I tried to sort by whether the keeper chose the post (923 "never chose to live there—they were chosen by it"; 8523 accept an offer; 13101 volunteers; 11235 enslaved; 15349 "They are not trapped."). Too few texts say either way (about 15) to make it a kind, but those that do say it are among the strongest answers.
- **Dropped: "hereditary".** 5433, 923, 16543, 1772, 16789 make the cost run through generations; folded into LIFEWORK/BODY.
- **Dropped: "cost stated as cost".** I considered a kind for texts that use an explicit word of price or giving (1755, 13319, 1974 "have taken a toll", 15157 "I have given much to this place", 1772 "have given their lives", 2512 "sacrifice", 16081 "surrendered all the messy liberties of life", 2383 "surrendering their physical forms", 14828 "surrendered shouting", 15656 "what a repeated mark costs"). About 10 beings. It cuts across all kinds, so I report it here instead.
- **The question itself.** For perhaps a third of the sample the text does not treat anything as a cost; the reader model picked the nearest sentences about limitation, strain or strangeness. My kinds therefore describe "what the texts say when asked for a cost", which is wider than "what the texts say the cost is".
