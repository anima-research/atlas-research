# fate_A: reading notes (reader A)

Source: `fate_A.md`, 300 beings, read whole, in file order. Question: what becomes of the keeper / tender / guardian.

How the counts were made: I read everything first, wrote down the kinds I had seen, then went through the 300 beings again and gave each one or more kinds. The lists below are those assignments; every count is the length of its list. A being may be in several kinds: 189 beings got one kind, 84 got two, 24 got three, 3 got four. The "not death but ..." wording (its own section after kind 15) is a feature of phrasing and is counted separately from the kinds.

## 1. They stay (87)

The text says the being endures, remains, continues the work, waits, does not age, is eternal. No end is described. In 58 beings this is the whole answer; in the other 29 it stands next to something else.

Two tones are visible inside it and I could not separate them cleanly enough to count: a generic closing sentence ("they will endure"), and a specific, often bleak picture of a keeper who cannot stop.

Generic:
- "And so they endure, as they always have." (2908)
- "They are the guardians of the land, the protectors of its balance and harmony, and they will continue to watch over the plateau, their presence a testament to the enduring power of nature." (9983)
- "And though the city beyond may crumble and fade, the Guardian and its enchanted courtyard will endure" (126)
- "And they will continue to do so, long after you have left." (9285)

Specific:
- "The Tender has no memory of anything but the job, and so it alone among everything down there has nothing left to mourn, and nothing left to fear losing, and goes on, plate over plate, joint by failing joint, holding the dark together from underneath." (13317)
- "They will always wait in the cold. They are waiting now, and this moment will never end, because ending would require change, and change is what they were born to prevent." (6865)
- "The entry will read what it has read for forty years: CONTINUING." (14515)
- "What is known is that the keeper has outlasted three complete changes of staff, the replacement of the main pump, and an unsuccessful attempt to convert half the court to storage." (16790)
- "They are neither alive nor dead; they are simply the inevitable conclusion of a machine that forgot how to stop." (10333)
- "It endures because it is not trying to." (12408)

## 2. After death the body is taken into the place (67)

There is a death (or an end the text refuses to call death), and then the text says what happens to the body: the place takes it. Two versions.

**2a. The body becomes building material, a structural part, a tool (27).** Bones as anchor pegs, a corpse as scaffolding, tiles, wall, fencing, floor.
- "Within an hour, the keeper has become three square yards of high-tensile fencing, indistinguishable from the rest of the floor" (16137)
- "Within weeks, it is compost. Within years, it is structure. Within generations, it is the railing a child leans on, the walkway a tender crosses" (6215)
- "they harvest its stone tools, its vine-cords, and even its bones, which are scraped clean and wedged into rock crevices to serve as anchor-pegs for the living" (12633)
- "the dead keep the position they held in life: the same address, one floor down" (15895)
- "What they say, if they say anything, is that the person has gone into the repair. It is the highest compliment they have." (16440)
- "They treat their petrified ancestor as what it has become: a new piece of scaffolding." (12551)

## 3. While still alive they slowly turn into the place (44)

No death event. With age or with length of service the keeper roots, calcifies, is grown over, slows until it cannot be told from wall or stone; or the text says outright that keeper and kept are no longer two things.
- "Eventually, there will be no distinction between Chen and the garden, between the Tender and the tended. But that's still years away, maybe decades." (6919)
- "The very old ones, who no longer climb, sit at the centers of their groves with their fingers on the trunk, and the grove around them takes their shape, so that the oldest gardens look strangely like the people who made them." (16705)
- "They become indistinguishable from the wall—living statues of lime and basalt, breathing once every few hours" (16151)
- "The body becomes part of the body. The maintainer becomes the maintained." (6326)
- "Its gardeners walked until they became its skin." (13865)
- "Slowly becoming part of what lives there, the way the machines have become part of what lives there, the way anything does that stays long enough for the place to grow around it." (1927)

## 4. A trace stays (39)

After the keeper is dead or gone, something remains that is not the body-as-material: a name on a valve, a function-name, tools in circulation, a flattened string, a tone, a landmark, memory absorbed by a collective or by the water.
- "Memory attaches instead to functions: 'Grandfather, who found the hairline leak before the wall slumped,' becomes, in time, simply 'the one who listened well at the fifth curve.'" (11823)
- "A grave flattens its string, permanently, slightly. ... no one ever corrects them, because to tune the dead away would be a kind of second death." (16364)
- "Their name may be painted on a valve, attached to a graft, or spoken whenever an old machine behaves in the particular way they taught it to behave." (13369)
- "In this way, the dead remain navigational." (9293)
- "When they die, they are buried in the silt at the tree's foot, and it is said that the tone deepens a fraction for a season afterward." (16704)
- "Death does not make a person absent immediately. For a time they remain in the division of labor." (13540)

One text states the opposite and I kept it here as the counter-case: "They leave no permanent mark of individual existence, but their collective presence is written in every maintained pathway" (7956); likewise 6326: "completely, without memorial, without void" (6326 is not in this list because of that).

## 5. No death as such: fading, dispersing, dormancy, re-forming (31)

The being has no clear end. It thins and thickens, dissolves and reappears elsewhere, alternates activity and dormancy, or is said to be neither born nor to die. Mostly non-human, non-bodied beings (spirits, swarms, machine-things, energy patterns).
- "They **do not die**, but they **fade**. ... Then, after days or centuries, they disappear entirely—only to reappear in a different part of the city, in a new form" (3462)
- "When one fades, another brightens. When one disperses, another coalesces. They are a population the way a flame is a population" (13812)
- "For millennia, it might not exist at all, its substance and nascent mind dissolved into the measureless expanse of the cinnabar floor." (2794)
- "So they do not die; they branch." (3724)
- "they do not age or die in any conventional sense, but rather cycle through periods of greater and lesser cohesion" (1251)

## 9. Already gone (15)

The keeper is absent at the time of the telling. What is there is a coat, a kettle, a machine still running, a buried shelter; or the function has been taken over by the room.

- "It has absorbed the role so completely that no keeper is needed." (15304)

## 10. Existence is the work (15)

Not a fate in time but a condition: the being exists only as long as it tends, or only in the role. Stopping, leaving or a change in the place would end it.
- "A tender that stops tending simply stops being a tender." (13306)
- "They know only that they attend, that their attending is their being, that to stop attending would be to stop existing." (5741)
- "the practice is what they are, and to stop reciting would be to stop being, and there is no one left to give them permission for that" (6798)
- "A person is called by what they steward while they stand near it ... and when they step away, the name dissolves like breath in bright air." (5622)
- "To leave would mean to cease to be, at least in the form they now know." (1388)
- "Fail the tests and the vascular grid reclaims what you were holding." (14228)

Beings: 5622, 14659, 13306, 6782, 13010, 5741, 8882, 15029, 6798, 1756, 1388, 910, 15304, 12716, 14228

## 11. Waiting for something / an end is foretold (13)

## Writers and care words

**Care words.** This is the clearest pattern I saw. (A being is counted under every care word that appears in its "given" field.)

| word | beings | stay only (kind 1 alone) | body-into-place or living merge (2 or 3) |
|---|---|---|---|

| keeper | 106 | 22 | 38 |

| gardener | 49 | 3 | 25 |

Guardians and custodians mostly endure, watch and are eternal; their bodies are hardly ever mentioned (kind 2: guardian 3 of 50, custodian 5 of 33). Gardeners and tenders mostly end up in what they tended (kind 2: gardener 17 of 49, tender 14 of 44) and almost never just "endure". Keeper sits between and is the word for the solitary post-holder: most of the already-gone cases (7 of 15) and of the doubt cases (5 of 6) are keepers. Guardian is also where "waiting / foretold event" clusters by rate (5 of 50). Traces (kind 4) go with gardener (12 of 49) and keeper (15 of 106), rarely with guardian (2 of 50).

Caution: "guardian" is given more often as a description of non-person beings (trees, relics, plants, phantoms) and by older or smaller models, so word and writer are tangled; I cannot separate them from this sample.
