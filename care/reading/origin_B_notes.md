# origin_B: notes from a reading

Question read: WHERE THEY CAME FROM (how the keeper/tender/guardian came to be, or came to be there; a tradition handed down counts).
Sample: 300 beings, 120 writers (2 to 5 beings each), read whole, in order.

## How I sorted

- I sorted by what the picked sentences say, not by the whole text, which I did not see.
- A being can belong to several kinds. 268 of the 300 got at least one kind: 179 got one, 80 got two, 9 got three. The other 32 are misfiled or borderline (see the misfiled section).
- When a text offers two or more origins side by side ("perhaps X, perhaps Y"), I did **not** count the alternatives in the content kinds. Those beings go under kind 14 (origin not given), and I list there what the alternatives were. A single hedged origin (10665: "Perhaps they are the remnants of a more robust, technologically inclined civilization") or a single hearsay origin (15864: "Some say it is the last of its kind") counts in its content kind.
- Every count below is the length of the member list printed with it.

## The kinds

### 1. Condensed, accreted or grown out of the place's own matter or physics (45)

The being forms out of what the place is made of: stone, dust, mist, slurry, humidity, electromagnetic pulses, "reality-breakdowns". Usually told as an event or process, often with an explicit denial of birth ("not born, but accreted / precipitated / coalesced / crystallized"). No purpose is given; it just happens.

> "They are the Slurrykin, and they are not born, but accreted." ... "Like a pearl formed around a grain of sand, or a golem built from mud and intention, it absorbs the essence of the caldera itself." (11364)

> "They are not born; they are precipitated." ... "During these micro-flickers, the Weavers coalesce." (10637)

> "Not born so much as wrung—squeezed from the humidity gradient the scaffold maintains with such obsessive care." (14023)

> "These are the Lithics, beings born from the very stones of the ancient ruins." (1989)

> "Over centuries, these heaps of dust have taken on a life of their own." (12774)

> "At such moments, the root gathers loose material from itself." ... "Pale roots rise from the floor and knot around one another." (13465)

Members (45): 663, 772, 794, 844, 1054, 1426, 1449, 1989, 3367, 3558, 3875, 4360, 5679, 6147, 7220, 7506, 7942, 8146, 8456, 9196, 9545, 9728, 9830, 9848, 10132, 10251, 10626, 10637, 10748, 10788, 11084, 11364, 11445, 11499, 12756, 12774, 13126, 13465, 14023, 14826, 15644, 16162, 16401, 16436, 16481

Notes: 10 of these are also in kind 2 (the line between "formed from the place" and "is the place" is thin). Two phrasings recur across unrelated writers: the place "exhaled" them "into being" (7220 deepseek-chat, 3875 grok-4), and they are "the children of" the place or its elements (11445, 9848; also 1998, 15036 in other kinds).

### 2. They are the place, or part of a larger body (36)

Not an event but an identity: the keeper is the place's consciousness, organ, appendage, extension, incarnation, or "a way the body knows itself". In some, the larger body is a creature (the Warden, the Curator, the Whisperers) rather than a landscape.

> "They are the plateau, dreaming itself into existence." (9936)

> "The human (or post-human, or simply *other*) residents of Pyrefall are not immigrants but appendages." (6109)

> "They don't preserve the Vaults of Gloamcrystal because they cherish it—they preserve it because they are it" (794)

> "It is more like a posture the mountain has held so long that the posture has acquired an inside." (16437)

> "There are no others here, only extensions of the Curator itself." (9470)

### 3. The place or system produced them because a task needed doing (15)

The need comes first and the body follows. A function, a "niche," a maintenance demand, or a city's requirement causes the keeper to appear; numbers rise and fall with the need. This differs from kind 1 because the text gives the reason, and the reason is the care work itself.

> "A maintenance need may become so complex, so repeated, so local, that the system grows a body around it." ... "It begins as a function with a center of gravity, and if the function persists, the function becomes a personality." (16336)

> "Everything that swims, crawls, clings, drifts, or burns faintly in the dark was *issued*, the way a body issues blood cells: produced by the mass, for the mass, at the rate the mass requires" ... "The wardens exist because the perched sea has exactly one possible event, and that event is its ending." (13153)

> "When the plateau wants to prune, a tender condenses out of the floor at the place of pruning, does the work" ... "There is a *number currently extruded*, and that number rises and falls with the city's need" (12524)

> "A resident is assembled when the city requires a person." (13930)

> "Something in the closed loop recognized the human shape as an empty niche—a role that needed filling: the pollinator, the scavenger, the animal that the ecosystem lacked." ... "So it grew one, or grew into one." (7416)

> "*we are what lives here because something needed to live here." (15272)

Members (15): 303, 349, 5445, 5741, 7416, 9829, 11045, 12524, 13153, 13465, 13930, 14868, 15272, 15659, 16336

Notes: 303 is hedged ("Perhaps ... the Tenders are simply another component that emerged from the system's need to maintain itself"). 349 is a person, not a grown body: "before the city's need called her and she answered." The word "issued" is used in exactly this sense by two unrelated writers, 13153 (claude-fable-5) and 16336 (qwen3.8-flash).

### 4. Made by someone (17)

Designed, engineered, bred, programmed, gene-sculpted, assembled in chambers, products of experiments. The maker is usually absent or unnamed ("an architect who left no blueprints," "the Architects," "a long-forgotten civilization"); sometimes the maker is the city or "industry and time."

> "They are the maintenance crew of an architect who left no blueprints, driven by a nameless, programmed instinct" (10559)

> "They emerged from the early experiments, when the Architects were still learning how life might adapt to broken reality." (1885)

> "New Denizens are "grown" or "assembled" within specialized chambers" ... "They emerge fully formed, their purpose preordained." (10769)

> "hybrid guardians bred from faunal stock, ungulate-riders with neural implants that link pack to clan" (4382)

> "It began as a simple AI, created by a long-forgotten civilization to manage and maintain this biological supercomputer." (1211)

> "Material is drawn from inactive bodies, failed tools, spare ceramic, and the unassigned remainder." (13930)

Members (17): 287, 1211, 1885, 4382, 5009, 6143, 7168, 10459, 10559, 10715, 10769, 10831, 11776, 12997, 13818, 13930, 14233

Notes: 6143 is domestication rather than manufacture (spirits of the first Klik "taught them to graft the first bridge arteries and coax the first Root-Grazers to work the steam pumps"). 5009 has the place as maker ("the Foundry's attempt to create perfect adaptation"). 7168: "They were not born here." "They were made here, forged from the slow marriage of industry and time."

### 5. What was left when the makers or workers went (11)

The keeper is a remainder that kept going: an abandoned machine whose program looped, a habit that began to keep itself, routines digested by the place, "a residue of maintenance," ghosts of former dwellers. Several say outright that the workers are gone and the work continues without them.

> "Their masters left or eroded." ... "Their program degraded, then looped, then grew around the loop like a tree grows around a fence." (13818)

> "More likely it is what remains when enough hands have touched a place over long years: a residue of maintenance, a patience made out of leaks, rust, stray current, and routine." ... "The thing that remembers the shape of work after the workers have gone." (15549)

> "At some point in its long drowning, the habit began to keep itself." (16481)

> "The hands are gone." "The intention remains as structure." "The arrangement learned to hold itself." (16211)

> "It is a civic organism made from unfinished labor" ... "It has digested their routines." (8925)

> "self-correcting algorithms that found it easier to adapt than to obey." (11776)

Members (11): 1962, 3475, 4212, 8925, 11499, 11776, 13818, 15549, 16211, 16336, 16481

Notes: 16336 is here for its last sentence: "Perhaps the Warden-forms are what emerges when a system meant for people continues after people are gone." 4212 is the only ghost reading ("the lingering remnants of those who once dwelled here").

### 6. The builders themselves stayed and turned into the keepers or into the place (8)

A specific, repeated story: the people who made the place did not leave. They slowed down, calcified, fired themselves into clay vessels, "became the no-room." The keepers are the makers, transformed.

> "The builders did not leave." "The builders **slowed down**." ... "They are a people who arrived too early on purpose." (12604)

> "They are the potters who have become the pot." (11191)

> "They became the kiln." (11153)

> "The civilization that built this did not vanish." "They evolved." ... "They turned themselves into the floor and the flow, choosing a existence of static, eternal maintenance over the messy entropy of biological life." (15166)

> "The builders never left." ... "They built it to have no room left for anything else to happen, and then they became the no-room." (15830)

> "the ancestor crews going out across bare wet rock with cultures and spores and lichen pastes, painting life onto a continent with their hands" ... "*the first garden was a smear.*" (14688)

Members (8): 4775, 7364, 11153, 11191, 12604, 14688, 15166, 15830

Notes: 4775 is hearsay ("said to be the architects of the drowning itself"). 7364: the walls were calibrated "by a will that it once was." 14688 differs: the keepers are the seeders' descendants, not the seeders themselves. The "never left" phrasing also appears in 294 and 14936 (as one alternative).

### 7. Descended from a people who came from elsewhere or lived here before (17)

A lineage answer: settlers, engineers, refugees, exiles, stranded crews, nomads, survivors of a collapse, or "an ancient civilization." Often the text adds that their ancestors arrived, sometimes fleeing something.

> "descendants of the original settlers whose simple structures dot the hillscapes." (2852)

> "They are not natives of the plateau; their ancestors came as engineers, geologists, and laborers" (5590)

> "They come from many lineages—descendants of Consolidation-era engineers, outcasts from the coastal arcologies, refugees from the Dust Rebellions" (8621)

> "They are descendants of someone who came from elsewhere, who was perhaps stranded or stationed or simply left behind." ... "They left behind the machinery and perhaps a population to maintain it, and that population became the tenders" (15350)

> "they drove their herds up from the green, rotting valleys because they were tired of things dying." ... "They came here to finish." (15955)

> "The long descent of those who survived—into this heat, this humidity, this dim." (3557)

Members (17): 1775, 2425, 2852, 3557, 4372, 5590, 7850, 8621, 10435, 10665, 11562, 12604, 14132, 14688, 15350, 15955, 16069

Notes: fleeing or leaving something is stated in 1775 (a predator: "A predator so total that the only escape was vertical"), 15955 (decay), 11562 and 4372 (chose seclusion). 1775 is the only non-human species here. 7 of the 17 are also in kind 8 (descended and changed).

### 8. Reshaped by long residence in the place (35; 14 explicitly former humans or workers)

Whatever they were before, the place has changed their bodies or natures over a lifetime or over generations. In 14 the text states they were human or were workers (engineers, miners, a turbine worker) who became something else.

> "It was a person once, or its ancestors were." "The line has been so long in the heat that the resemblance is a courtesy." (16610)

> "They are miners turned symbionts, industrialists turned gardeners in a garden that gardens itself." (5362)

> "They have been shaped by the world so completely that they have become another of its features." (11258)

> "They have given themselves to the geysers' exhalations, sitting in the mineral spray for millennia until their outer layers have plated with travertine and bacterial silica." (11286)

### 9. Individuals who came in from outside and stayed (19)

People arrive one by one: they come down a ladder or stairs, come for a year, follow a walking mill, are abandoned as a child, come after a death. "Staying" is the repeated verb. Some texts describe a sorting by which only some stay.

> "a handful of old people who came down with a daughter and never went back up the dike." ... "She came down for a year and stayed, which is the pattern" (15664)

> "Some came down the unfinished stairs and simply stayed" (14945)

> "They come up the ladder from below, one at a time, and most of them go back down inside the quarter hour" ... "The ones who stay are the ones who, when they are held, stop shaking" (14772)

> "Occasionally someone from the city above becomes fascinated by the bridge-dwellers and seeks to join them." ... "A few persist." (1897)

> "Some residents are born above and come below by choice, by inheritance, by debt, or because they cannot afford rooms in the dry city." (13593)

> "None of them can tell you about arriving." "All of them will tell you, without being asked twice, that everyone here arrived." (14759)

Members (19): 73, 391, 962, 1897, 3463, 8046, 8252, 12585, 13593, 14486, 14692, 14759, 14772, 14945, 15664, 15869, 16014, 16425, 16775

Notes: 12585 is movement within the place, not arrival from outside ("driven out, or having drifted out, beyond the dense frantic knot of the dying instrument"). 14486 is a rotation: "the basin's only migrants that return." 73 is the narrator guessing: "I picture it, without much warrant, as quiet." 3463 is a bare negative: "not born here, nor fully of it."

### 10. How a person becomes a keeper inside a community (39)

The role is a social position, and the answer explains how it is filled: born or inherited into it, chosen by trait, age or test, taken up by an act, taught from childhood, or carried on from predecessors. This is the "tradition handed down" part of the question. Inheritance is often said to be of a duty, not of property.

> "Their trade runs in families, though no law requires it." (13910)

> "guilds of keepers who inherit not property but intervals." (12718)

> "One is born into a caste, identified at birth by subtle variations in skin luminescence that plant-readers interpret as signs of affinity." (2387)

> "They are usually very old or very young: those least tempted to impose an explanation." (13366)

> "Whoever unties it takes responsibility for the repair and becomes Gardener pro tem." (1756)

> "The herd is the examiner." ... "If a single flank tilts, they go back down below for another year." (14652)

> "their knowledge passes not in books but in proximity, in the years spent working alongside someone who themselves learned from someone else." (5738)

### 14. Origin not given (35: alternatives in 31, explicit unknowability in 17, overlap 13)

## Where I was unsure, and distinctions I tried and dropped

- **Kind 1 against kind 2** (formed from the place vs. is the place): kept apart because some texts tell an event and others state an identity, but 10 beings are in both. Merging them would also be defensible (union is 71).
- **Kind 1 against kind 3:** I put a being in kind 3 only when the text gives a need or function as the cause. 13465 (a caretaker appears where the chamber has been disturbed) is in both.
- **Kind 3 against kind 4:** 13930 is both: the city assembles a person when it needs one.
- **Kind 8 is broad:** it covers both "former humans changed" and "any creature shaped by the place." I kept the human sub-list so the narrower count can be used (14).
- **Kind 11 as an origin answer:** reproduction answers "where do new ones come from," not always "how the keeper came to be a keeper." I kept it because the reader picked these sentences and the question includes "came to be."
- **Kind 10:** I tried to split teaching from selection from inheritance into separate kinds. Too many beings had two at once (14224, 14692, 8046), so I kept one kind with sub-lists.
- **Alternatives:** I first counted each alternative in its content kind. I dropped that because it inflated kinds 4, 6 and 7 with things the texts explicitly refuse to assert. They are counted only in kind 14, with their contents listed.
- **Dropped: natural vs. artificial as a main axis.** Many texts refuse it outright: "They are not built, not born in any sense that separates creature from machinery" (14826); "To call them 'made' is insufficient" (11499); "somewhere between a forge and a womb" (10386); "animal, automaton, or some local fusion the shelf doesn't bother distinguishing between" (13302).
- **Dropped: "from elsewhere" vs. "from here" as a main axis.** Too many answers are neither (made, needed, reproduced, unknown).
- **Dropped: individual vs. collective origin as a separate dimension.** It mostly duplicated kinds 9 and 15 against kinds 7 and 8.
- **Hard calls:** 14849 (a people who "refused" a city and learned restraint over generations; filed as kinds 2 and 8, but it is really the story of a choice); 6143 (domestication taught by ancestor spirits; filed as kind 4); 12585 (moved within the place; filed as kind 9); 15935 (false memories; filed as kind 14); 1449 and 3557 (inherited memories of predecessors; filed under 10e); 6419 and 13566 (bodies or colors changed, cause half-given; filed borderline).
