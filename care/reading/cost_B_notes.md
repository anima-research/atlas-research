# cost_B — reading notes: "what it costs them"

Reader B. Sample: `cost_B.md`, 300 beings, 120 writers (1–5 beings each), 936 picked sentences (mean 3.1 per being). I read the whole file once from start to end and took notes on each being. Then I gave each being one or more kinds by hand, and counted the lists with a script. Every count below comes from the per-being table at the end of this file (Appendix A). A being can belong to several kinds: 137 beings have one kind, 93 have two, 58 have three or more, and 12 have none (the wholly misfiled ones).

Quote format: `[being] "verbatim sentence"`. A " / " separates two sentences from the same being, and " ... " marks a cut inside a sentence. I checked every quote against the source file with a script after writing.

## 1. The kinds of answer, by count

### 1.1 The body pays: marks, wear, injury, pain — 65

The work leaves something on or in the body: calluses, scars, stains, cracked hands, bad knees and lungs, deafness, eyes damaged by glare, ageing early, acute pain. The body stays recognisably the same kind of body, only marked. This is the largest kind.

- [8934] "Their bodies bear the city’s hazards." / "Scars from steam." / "Flattened fingertips from metalwork." / "Knees made bad by stairs." / "Hearing shaped by turbines."
- [16131] "They are completely deaf, their tympanic membranes having ruptured in childhood or been intentionally pierced with brass pins to spare them the agony of the relief valves."
- [15809] "Their lungs are bad — years of boiler sulfur and lime steam and spore."
- [16758] "There are kiln-tenders, who go grey-lashed and half-deaf in the dry heat and are envied and pitied for it."
- [15604] "Their hands are often stained white."
- [15649] "And the old ones wear out like the bearings."

A split inside this kind that I think matters: in about 27 of the 65, the text gives the mark as plain description and has no word of loss, harm or complaint. Examples are "hands are often stained white", "palms are ridged with rope-callus from childhood" and "soles of their feet have hardened into a pale, thick leather". These 27 are 9347, 1044, 15604, 2624, 16280, 13797, 7657, 4046, 3186, 13385, 5341, 9571, 15937, 8371, 16412, 3636, 10555, 16205, 16577, 6667, 5738, 13945, 16743, 702, 16649, 14942 and 12664. In these the text does not call the mark a cost; the reader model read it as one. In the other ~38 the text marks it as harm itself, with words like "hazards", "agony", "lungs fail", "crush a finger" or "wear out". One of them goes further than any other: [6667] says outright that the wearing is not a loss: "not eroded into absence, but rounded into a final shape".

### 1.4 The body is changed into something else (not damage, a change of kind) — 31

The place or the work turns the body into another kind of body. Examples: translucent skin, webbing, slower pulse, no hunger, no flight, protective deafness, "not quite human baseline", "less like warm mammals". The difference from 1.1 is that this is a change of kind, not a mark; the difference from 1.8 is that the being stays separate from the place.

### 1.7 Alone: solitude, loneliness, few of them, cut off from others — 30

The keeper is the only one, or one of very few, or separated from the town. This kind is mixed: some texts name loneliness plainly, and seven deny it in so many words (see 2.1). I also put texts that only say the keepers are very few here; that is a fact about the group rather than a lonely person, and I note the doubt in section 6.

### 1.8 Becoming part of the place or of what they keep — 30

The keeper fuses with, roots into, is grown over by, or is absorbed into the place or the machine. The line between keeper and kept goes away. In many of these texts this happens to the eldest: in 2625, 4415, 8634, 10182, 238, 4615, 553, 14822, 410 and 6706, and arguably 10417 and 6667, merging is how a keeper's life ends. So it reads as an old-age outcome more than a cost paid all along.

- [8159] "They have been down there so long that their lower bodies have fused with the valves, becoming biological gaskets, their flesh pulsing in counter-rhythm to the pump-cycles, filtering, warming, *remembering*."
- [238] "They have become part of the architecture, their tendrils so interwoven with the root systems that it becomes impossible to say where Gardener ends and tree begins."
- [6667] "At some point a tender stops walking the stairs and becomes part of the wall it was walking past, and the work it was doing continues to be done, through it, by it, as it."
- [16003] "To move one would require breaking the basalt upon which it stands."
- [9585] "These were the Curators, architects of the fusion, who solved the problem of maintenance by making the maintainer obsolete." / "They did not die; they were absorbed."

### 1.19 Chosen self-limitation — 8

They limit themselves on purpose. They take little, leave no mark, own nothing, do not finish things, eat only what the place can spare, give up pride, or hold their numbers down. What separates this from 1.18 is that the text says the choice is theirs.

## 6. Where I was unsure, and distinctions I tried and dropped

- **Mark vs change of kind (1.1 vs 1.4).** Deafness from pumps went to 1.1 and "protective deafening" (13286) to 1.4. Eyes "filmed silver" (13503) and salt-cured skin (16712) could go either way. "less physically resilient" (8044) went to 1.4 as a lessened capacity.
- **Change of kind vs becoming the place (1.4 vs 1.8).** "skin has taken on the color of the walls" (6545) and hands that "darken green ... as though the place has signed them" (15343) could be marking or merging. I put 6545 in 1.1 and 15343 in both 1.4 and 1.8.
- **Becoming the place vs loss of self vs death.** [9585] "They did not die; they were absorbed." Absorption, dissolution and ending overlap. I double-coded rather than force a choice.
- **Endless vs bound vs futile.** "To cease operating is to invite ... annihilation" (10092) is about endlessness and bondage at once; I coded it endless only. Futility is reserved for texts that say the work changes nothing or the wait is for nothing.
- **Alone vs few.** "They are remarkably few in number" (16164) and "They are few" (14942) describe the group; "terribly alone" (14996) describes a person. I kept them together. Splitting them would give 5 that are only "few" (16164, 12961, 14255, 14942, 7676) and 25 others.
- **Slowness as trait vs cost.** I counted slowness only when the text frames it as painful, imposed or a change, and sent the rest to weak or misfiled. This is the softest line in the sorting.
- **The observer's experience.** [11177] "almost painful to a creature of faster metabolism" describes the visitor's pain. [1782] describes the visitor's discomfort. I counted 11177 under tempo and marked 1782's sentences as misfiled. I am not confident either way.
- **Imposed vs chosen.** I tried sorting every cost by whether the being chose it. I dropped this because most texts do not say. Only 1.19 keeps the distinction, because there the texts say so outright.
- **Lifelong vs end-of-life.** I tried a separate kind for costs that come only in old age. I dropped it as a kind and noted it inside 1.8 instead, where it clusters.
- **Individual vs the whole kind.** "their very *kind* was temporary" (9870), a race "in twilight" (6347) and a static population (2085) are costs to a people. I kept them inside death and self-limitation rather than making a kind.
- **"No inner life" vs "no cost".** I kept these apart (1.x smaller kind vs 2.1). The no-wants texts do not argue that nothing is lost; they report an absence.
- **Natural lifespan vs a death caused by the role.** A ten-day moth (8866) and a two-year isopod (5428) are just lifespans. I flagged 8866 as weak and kept 5428 because it adds "die without seeing its young".
