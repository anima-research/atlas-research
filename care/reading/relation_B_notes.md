# Relation, sample B: reading notes

Question: how the keeper / tender / guardian (etc.) stands to what or whom it cares for.
File read: `relation_B.md`, 300 beings, 1,376 filed sentences, 124 writers. I read the whole file in order. I did not open any other file in the directory.

## How the counts were made

While reading I gave each being free-form tags with a one-line note (300 lines, kept in my scratchpad). When the whole file was read, I grouped the tags into kinds. Then I went through each kind's list by hand against my notes, adding and removing beings. Every count below is the length of the list printed under it. A being can be in several kinds: the average is about 3, and 60 beings fall into exactly one. I put the kinds into families only to make the report easier to read. The families are not claims.

"Fusion", "organ" and the other labels are my shorthand. The quotes are verbatim and carry the being number.

## Family A: where the boundary between keeper and kept lies

### A1. Fusion: the keeper *is* the place, or there is no line between them (75)

This is the single most frequent answer. The keeper is the city or chamber. Or the place is its body, or the two "cannot be separated." Variants: "gardener and garden" formulas; resemblance so complete that copying stops meaning anything; the keeper's nerves or blood running through the walls.

- "They are the city, and the city is them." (2036)
- "The city is their body, and their bodies are the city’s material." (9744)
- "In this way the inhabitants are both gardeners and garden, continually edited into the place that edits them." (14023)
- "A caretaker *becomes* the place, or the place becomes the caretaker, and eventually the distinction dissolves." (6455)
- "The city is their body, enlarged to a valley’s size, kept wet, kept warm, kept full, kept always unfinished, and therefore always alive." (16338)
- "They are simply *the same* as where they live, the way old groundskeepers eventually become the color of the soil they work." (8071)

### B3. Motive stated: why they do it (23)

There is a small sub-kind in which **the care is not needed by the cared-for**: 6100, 14325, 14936, 15688. Examples: gears that never wear, bolts that never loosen, a reef that needs "only expression," a city that "does not need" the care.

## A form across kinds: definition by negation (72)

## Where I was unsure, and distinctions I tried and dropped

- **Fusion vs organ.** I kept them apart because "they are the city" and "they are its white blood cells" are different claims (whole vs part, equal vs subordinate). 23 beings make both. A reader who merges them gets 92 (the union of A1 and A2). Adding becoming and self-keeping gives 97.
- **Reciprocity vs reversal.** I first had one "two-way" kind. I split it because reversal texts often say the keeper is *kept* (16381) or held up (15645), and reciprocity texts do not. Only 6 overlap.
- **Restraint vs guest vs tolerance of other life.** These blur. I first had one "non-interference" kind. I separated acts of self-limitation (D1), standing by permission (D2) and treatment of secondary life (D3) because they come apart in texts like 16617, which has all three, or 5365, which has only D2. 10 beings are in both D1 and the tolerate list of D3.
- **Sacred vs fear.** 5997 ("reverence bordering on fear") and 315 ("terror or worship") sit on the line. I put appeasement under D6 (ruled by) and fear-tinged reverence under E4.
- **Indifference.** I first had one "indifferent keeper" kind, then saw that most of it is indifference to the *visitor* (G1), not to what is kept. Only 4 keepers are indifferent to the life they keep (D3).
- **"Told from the other side."** I tried a separate kind for answers given from the cared-for's side, like 8312 ("people and animals alike orient themselves to it") or 16376. I dropped it and spread those beings over A5 (ground/host), B2 (reversal) and G2 (standing).
- **Object type** (keeping a place, a machine, creatures, a memory). I considered sorting by what is kept and did not code it, since the question is about stance. I have no counts for it.
- **Plain work (C4)** is so broad that I nearly dropped it. I kept it because for 14 beings it is the whole answer.
- **Generic vs weak.** I kept "stock phrasing" (G3) apart from "off-question" (misfiled). A stock sentence is still an answer; an off-question sentence is not.
- **Hard individual calls.**
  - 2313 (made of echoes): fusion or nothing.
  - 9310 (tends with her body): fusion or work. I chose work.
  - 13952 (rooted to walls in sleep): fusion or ground. I chose ground.
