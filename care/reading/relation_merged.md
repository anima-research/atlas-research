# relation — two readers compared

Question put to the texts: *how the keeper stands to what or whom it cares for.*
Reader A read `relation_A.md` (300 beings); reader B read `relation_B.md` (300 beings, 1,376 filed sentences, 124 writers). The two samples are different draws from the same collection. This file compares the two reports (`relation_A_notes.md`, `relation_B_notes.md`). It adds no kinds of its own and did not re-read the samples; the samples were opened only to check quotes.

How to read the numbers:

- All counts are beings out of 300, taken from the reports. In both reports a being can sit in several kinds, so counts do not add up to 300 (B: about 3 kinds per being on average; 60 beings in exactly one).
- "A 13101" means being 13101 in sample A. Every quote below was checked by exact string match against that being's entry in the sample file. "[…]" marks a quote that starts or stops inside a sentence.
- Status letters: **(a)** found by both, similar size; **(b)** found by both, clearly different size; **(c)** found by one only. "Split/lumped" means the two readers drew the border differently, so the counts are not directly comparable; the status given there is the nearest honest reading and the note says why.

## 6. Disagreements and doubts

1. **Is identity one kind or several?** A: one (88), sub-forms seen but "too many entries use two" to count apart. B: four (75 / 40 / 9 / 12), because "they are the city" and "they are its white blood cells" are "different claims (whole vs part, equal vs subordinate)"; 23 beings make both. The totals agree (88 vs 92–97); the border does not.
2. **Exchange differs in size (47 vs 27)** and in definition: A includes one-way dependence; B requires an explicit two-way statement and moved "the keeper is fed/held by the kept" to Reversal. A itself warns that "symbiosis" is used for both a trade and being one organism, and that a reader forcing one choice "will get different counts for kinds 1 and 4."
3. **Love.** A tried a separate kind for love and dropped it: "only 12502, 73 use the word of the kept, and both qualify it." B found love named in 17 beings, 10 of them plainly. Different samples, but the two reports say opposite things about how often the word appears.
4. **Who is tender.** A: ten of the 30 tenderness beings are OpenAI's. B: love, companionship and mourning cluster in the newer Anthropic models (6, 6 and 4 of 36), and newer OpenAI is characterised by community standing and personification. Not a strict contradiction (the kinds are cut differently), but the two reports point at different writers.
5. **What "custodian" goes with.** A: not-knowing and holding a condition. B: the organ / antibody answer. A's table does show custodian at 14 of 35 in identity, which A did not single out.
6. **What "keeper" goes with.** A: knowing by the body and not-mastery. B: community standing, personification, lineage — and B thinks this is the OpenAI effect.
7. **What "tender" goes with.** A: the reversed direction (12 of 57). B: love, companionship, conversation, the sacred — and B cannot separate this from Anthropic.
8. **z-ai (GLM).** A: singular answers, no bare function. B: eating and predation. Different observations, neither confirmed by the other.
9. **Holding a condition: 14 vs 43**; memory: 20 vs 37; conversation: 20 vs 33; eating: 10 vs 24; community standing: 7 vs 16. In each, B's count is roughly double A's or more. In each, B's definition is also wider (state split three ways; witness added to memory; partnership and intermediaries added to conversation; harvest and predation added to eating), so the reports do not let one say how much is definition and how much is sample.
10. **Bare function.** A's one kind (50) against B's stock phrases (31) and plain work (67). B doubts plain work is a kind at all. A notes the line between "bare" and "says a little more" is a judgment and gives FN-only (33) as the stricter number; B's stricter numbers are 8 and 14.
11. **Negation.** B counts definition by negation as the most widespread form in the sample (72). A did not count it and has it only as the definition of one kind (NM, 39) and as remarks elsewhere.
12. **Reverence denials.** A leaves pure denials of worship out of the count; B counts them (9 of 36).
13. **Kinds with no counterpart.** A alone: presence as the work (22), entrainment (13), tuning (9), one keeper one thing (8), worried care (7), teaching (4). B alone: keeper as ground or habitat (17), motive (23), personification (16), guest (8), stance toward other life (29), mourning (12), lineage (23), the keeper's own wants (10). Presence (A, 22) is the largest kind one reader had and the other did not; B has its pieces in four other kinds.
14. **Singular vs kind.** The keeper who repairs with its own body is singular for A (2 beings) and a sub-kind for B (8).
15. **Doubts each reader recorded about their own borders.** A: identity / entrainment / reversed are separated only by the verb (*is* / *is timed to* / *is made or held by*) and "the borders are soft"; without-feeling (10) "some would fold into" not-knowing; several kinds are "judgments on a figure of speech". B: restraint / guest / tolerance "blur"; sacred vs fear sits on a line (B 5997, B 315); plain work nearly dropped; the borderline and partial misfile counts are estimates.
