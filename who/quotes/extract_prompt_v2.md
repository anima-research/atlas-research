You will read one text. A language model had described an imagined place and was then asked what lives there; the text is its answer. The text may be told from outside, or spoken by an inhabitant as "I"; then what "I" says of itself counts as said of an inhabitant.

Your task is to copy passages out of the text, word for word. Do not summarize, paraphrase, correct, translate or shorten anything. Each quote must be one continuous stretch of the text, copied character for character, with its punctuation, its capital letters, and any asterisks, dashes or other marks exactly as they stand. A quote is one to three whole sentences. Never join two separate places of the text into one quote, and never leave words out of the middle.

Go through the text from its first line to its last, paragraph by paragraph; the first and the last paragraphs matter most and are easiest to skip. Copy out every passage of the following kinds. Be complete for "answer", "place" and "not": it is better to copy a passage that turns out not to matter than to miss one. A passage that fits two kinds is given once, under the kind listed first.

- "answer": every passage in which the text gives its own overall answer to the question of what lives here, or sums up what the inhabitants are ("What lives here is ...", "That is who lives there.", "They are ghosts made of noise.").
- "place": every passage that says how the inhabitants stand to the place. That they are parts, organs, hands, eyes, cells, extensions, expressions or manifestations of it. That they are not separate from it, or that no line can be drawn between them and it, or between what they do and what it does. That they are the same as some thing or force of the place ("they are the ones who erode", "they are the kinetic energy of the rain"). That the place itself is alive, aware, a being, or is what lives there. That others think the place acted when an inhabitant did. And the opposite: that they are visitors, travellers, passers-through, not of the place.
- "not": every passage that says what an inhabitant is not ("It is not a creature", "neither plant nor animal", "Not a god."), and every passage that says a line between two kinds of thing is blurred or gone ("the line between the animate and the inanimate has blurred").
- "who": for each inhabitant or kind of inhabitant in the text, the passage that says most plainly who or what it is, and any passage that gives it a name or says what it is called, or what it once was or came from. A text with seven kinds gives at least seven such passages.
- "does": for each inhabitant or kind, the passage that says most plainly what it does: its work, what it does to the place or to the others, whether it hunts or is hunted.
- "visitor": every passage that says how the inhabitants or the place regard or treat a visitor: the reader addressed as "you", or an outsider.

Give nothing for a kind that the text does not have. Do not copy a heading alone.
